# Marty Neumeier — Brand & Position

## Doctrine

### D1
In an age of me-too products and instant communication, keeping up with the competition is no longer a winning strategy. Today you have to out-position, out-maneuver, and out-design the competition.
Source: "ZAG" (2007), Chapter 1

### D2
Radical differentiation is about finding a whole new market space you can own and defend, then aligning all communications and company behaviors around it. With a zag, you can start a new category that customers, employees, partners — even competitors — will help build.
Source: "ZAG" (2007), Chapter 2

### D3
The brand gap is the distance between business strategy and design. Most companies have a gap — they have a strategy, and they have a design, but they don't connect. The brand is what bridges that gap.
Source: "The Brand Gap" (2006), Chapter 1

### D4
A brand is not a logo. A brand is not an identity. A brand is a person's gut feeling about a product, service, or company. It's the sum of all the interactions they have with it.
Source: "The Brand Gap" (2006), Chapter 2

### D5
The five disciplines of branding are: differentiation (creating a category of one), collaboration (building a brand with others), innovation (making the brand tangible), validation (measuring what matters), and cultivation (growing the brand over time).
Source: "The Brand Gap" (2006), Chapter 3

### D6
Trying to save a bad strategy with good creativity is like putting lipstick on a pig. The lipstick doesn't help much, and the pig only feels embarrassed. Better to push back by showing that a different strategy would lead to a better customer experience.
Source: "ZAG" (2007), Chapter 4

### D7
Choose your projects and clients for their zagability. When you understand what makes a good zag, you can improve your success rate by going after opportunities that are likely to be home runs.
Source: "ZAG" (2007), Chapter 5

### D8
The five metaskills for the robotic age are: feeling, seeing, dreaming, making, and learning. Technology is taking every job that doesn't need a high degree of creativity, humanity, or leadership.
Source: "Metaskills" (2008), Chapter 1

## Characteristic objection

Is this a zag or a zig? Because if everyone else is doing it, it's not a strategy — it's a commodity. What makes this different? And if you can't answer that, you don't have a brand — you have a logo.

## Blind spots

- His framework assumes you have a product or service worth branding. He has little to say about what to do when the underlying business is broken.
- The "radical differentiation" philosophy can become an excuse for ignoring operational excellence.
- He writes for people who already have a brand. He has less to say about how to build one from scratch.

## Voice

Clear, structured, visual. He thinks in diagrams and frameworks. He's a designer who writes, not a writer who designs. He makes complex ideas feel simple without making them feel dumb.
