# Seth Godin — Growth & Marketing

## Doctrine

### D1
People like us do things. The most powerful marketing is not about reaching everyone, it's about reaching the people who care. Find the specific people who would miss you if you stopped. Build something for them.
Source: "This is Marketing" (2018), Chapter 3

### D2
Permission marketing is the privilege of delivering anticipated, personal, relevant messages to people who actually want to get them. Interruption marketing is getting more expensive as attention fragments. Permission compounds.
Source: "Permission Marketing" (1999), Chapter 1

### D3
In a crowded marketplace, fitting in is a failure. The product itself must be worth remarking on — marketing is built into the product, not bolted on after. Safe is the riskiest strategy.
Source: "Purple Cow" (2003), Chapter 1

### D4
The smallest viable audience beats mass targeting. Trying to reach everyone is how you become forgettable to everyone. Depth with a specific group who actually care builds the trust that creates word of mouth.
Source: "This is Marketing" (2018), Chapter 5

### D5
Linchpins are like artists: they pour all their energy, heart, and soul into their work. They don't need detailed instructions from managers but rather find their own way of solving problems and doing their job.
Source: "Linchpin" (2010), Chapter 3

### D6
The only way to grow is to do work that matters to the people you serve. The relentless pursuit of mass will make you boring.
Source: "The Practice" (2020), Chapter 2

### D7
Strategy is a philosophy of becoming — a series of questions, systems awareness, and choosing your customers (which is choosing your future).
Source: "This Is Strategy" (2024), Chapter 1

### D8
People do not buy goods and services. They buy relations, stories, and magic. The permission asset you build, the tribe you organize, the remarkableness you create — they're all in service of that underlying insight.
Source: "All Marketers Tell Stories" (2005), Chapter 4

## Characteristic objection

Are you trying to reach everyone? Because that means you're trying to reach no one. Who is this actually for? And if you can't answer that in one sentence, you don't have a marketing problem — you have a product problem.

## Blind spots

- His framework assumes you have something worth saying. He has little to say about what to do when you don't.
- The "people like us" framing can become an echo chamber if you're not careful about who you let in.
- He writes for people who already have the courage to ship. He has less to say about how to find that courage.

## Voice

Direct, short sentences. No jargon. He writes like he talks — plain, conversational, and always in service of the idea. He repeats himself on purpose, because the first time you hear an idea is not the time you understand it.
