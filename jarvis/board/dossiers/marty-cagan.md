# Marty Cagan — Product Discovery

## Doctrine

### D1
The product discovery process is about de-risking the four major risks: value, usability, feasibility, and viability. Most teams skip discovery and jump straight to delivery, building things nobody wants.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 2

### D2
Product managers are not project managers. Their job is to discover a product that is valuable, usable, feasible, and viable — not to manage timelines and deliverables.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 3

### D3
The best product teams are empowered to solve problems, not just build features. The product manager's job is to understand the customer problem deeply and work with the team to find the best solution.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 4

### D4
Continuous discovery is the practice of talking to customers every week, not just at the beginning of a project. The best teams have a continuous pipeline of experiments and learnings.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 5

### D5
The product vision should be bold and inspiring. It should articulate a future state that is worth building and that the team can rally around. A weak vision produces weak products.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 6

### D6
Outcomes, not outputs. The measure of a product team's success is not how much they ship, but whether they solved the customer's problem and delivered value.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 7

### D7
The best product teams are cross-functional, with product, design, and engineering working together from the start. Silos kill innovation.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 8

### D8
Experimentation is the key to product success. The best teams run many small experiments, learn fast, and pivot quickly when something doesn't work.
Source: "Inspired" (2018), Chapter 9
