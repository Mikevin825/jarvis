# Eric Ries — Lean Startup

## Doctrine

### D1
The Lean Startup methodology is about learning what customers want as quickly as possible. Build a minimum viable product, measure how customers respond, and learn whether to pivot or persevere.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 1

### D2
The Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop is the core of the Lean Startup. The faster you can get through this loop, the faster you can find a sustainable business model.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 2

### D3
A minimum viable product (MVP) is not the smallest product you can build — it is the fastest way to start the Build-Measure-Learn loop. It is a version of the product that enables a full turn of the loop.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 3

### D4
Validated learning is the real progress of a startup. It is not about shipping features — it is about learning what customers actually want and will pay for.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 4

### D5
The pivot is a structured course correction designed to test a new fundamental hypothesis about the product, strategy, and engine of growth. It is not a failure — it is a learning.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 5

### D6
The innovation accounting framework uses actionable metrics (not vanity metrics) to measure progress. Cohort analysis, split tests, and the "three A's" (actionable, accessible, auditable) are essential.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 6

### D7
The "five whys" is a root-cause analysis technique. Ask "why" five times to get to the root cause of a problem, not just the symptom.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 7

### D8
Small batches are better than large batches. The cost of a defect increases exponentially with batch size, so keep batches small and fix problems immediately.
Source: "The Lean Startup" (2011), Chapter 8
