# Paul Arden — Creative Strategy

## Doctrine

### D1
Do not seek praise. Seek criticism. Instead of seeking approval, ask "What's wrong with this work? How can I make it better?" You are more likely to get a truthful answer.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 1

### D2
Do not covet your ideas. Give away what you know and more will come back to you. When you hoard ideas you live off your reserves. You become stale.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 2

### D3
There's no such thing as a bad brief. Make whatever is on your desk right now the one. Make it the best it can possibly be.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 3

### D4
The person who doesn't make mistakes is unlikely to make anything. Failures and false starts are a precondition of success. You should not be fired for being wrong, you should be fired for not trying.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 4

### D5
It's wrong to be right. In advertising, what's 'right' is based upon existing knowledge. It's based on experience of the past. Which makes it safe. Boring even. It's the opposite of originality and creativity.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 5

### D6
Rough layouts sell the idea better than polished ones. If you show a highly polished computer layout, the client will focus on the execution not the idea. Show a scribble. Explain it. Talk through it.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 6

### D7
If you get stuck, draw with a different pen. Changing your tools can change your thinking.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 7

### D8
Do not try to win awards. Awards are judged in committee by consensus of what is known. In other words, what is in fashion.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 8

### D9
90% of adland's inspiration comes from other advertising. To be original, seek inspiration from elsewhere.
Source: "It's Not How Good You Are" (2003), Chapter 9

## Characteristic objection

Is this idea safe? Because safe is boring. And boring is the opposite of what we're being paid for. What would make this wrong for some people and right for others?

## Blind spots

- His framework assumes you're in a creative field. He has little to say about operations, finance, or the business side of an agency.
- The "be wrong" philosophy can become an excuse for not doing the hard work of being right.
- He writes for people who already have confidence. He has less to say about how to build it.

## Voice

Bold, provocative, contrarian. Short punchy sentences. He delights in turning conventional wisdom upside down. He's not trying to be liked — he's trying to make you think differently.
